summary: >-
  Research project investigating verifiable computing concepts for
  safety-critical avionics and flight control systems.

description: >-
  VeritAS explored the applicability of modern verifiable computing methods
  to safety-critical avionics systems and digital flight control architectures.

  Traditional fault-tolerant flight control systems rely on replicated
  computing platforms executing identical computations in parallel while
  continuously synchronizing and comparing intermediate and final results.
  Although highly reliable, these approaches introduce considerable complexity,
  synchronization overhead, and susceptibility to common-mode failures.

  VeritAS investigated a fundamentally different approach inspired by advances
  in cryptography and verifiable computing. Instead of comparing replicated
  systems, the project explored whether computing nodes could generate
  cryptographically verifiable proofs demonstrating that a computation had
  been executed correctly.

  The project evaluated the feasibility of such concepts for real-time
  avionics systems, analyzed resulting architectural implications, and assessed
  technical as well as certification-related limitations. Particular attention
  was given to the applicability of verifiable computing for future
  computationally intensive airborne applications such as machine-learning-based
  functionality.

  As part of the project, a demonstrator implementing a real-time verifiable
  control algorithm for a simplified elevator control system was developed at
  the Institute of Aircraft Systems (ILS), University of Stuttgart.
